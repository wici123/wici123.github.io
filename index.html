🖱️ MacLinker - One Keyboard, Every Mac

Control several Macs with a single keyboard and mouse — no extra hardware, no dongles, no subscriptions. MacLinker is a free, open-source alternative to Universal Control that works over Wi-Fi, Ethernet or a USB-C cable, and keeps working even when your VPN is switched on.

🚀 What Is MacLinker?

If you own more than one Mac, you probably know the small annoyance of owning more than one keyboard and mouse too. You reach for the wrong one, you bump the wrong trackpad, and your desk slowly turns into a tangle of cables.

MacLinker fixes that. Install it on the computers you want to link together, and they begin to act like one setup. Move your mouse off the edge of one screen and it simply appears on the next Mac over. Type on one keyboard and the letters land wherever your mouse pointer is. Copy something on one machine and paste it on another. Drag a file across and it transfers over your own network instead of through a cloud service.

Everything runs locally between your own machines. There is no account to create, no monthly fee, and no data sent to a third-party server. MacLinker lives quietly in your menu bar, ready when you need it and out of the way when you don't.

Because it uses the network you already have, MacLinker doesn't care whether your Macs are side by side on a desk or in different rooms on the same home network. Wi-Fi, a wired Ethernet connection, or a direct USB-C cable between two machines all work.

✨ Key Features

⌨️ Keyboard Sharing

Type on one keyboard and control every linked Mac. Your text goes to whichever screen your pointer is on.

🖱️ Mouse Sharing

Slide your pointer off one screen edge and it continues onto the next Mac. No extra hardware or switch box needed.

📋 Clipboard Sync

Copy text or links on one Mac and paste them straight into an app on another. Your clipboard follows your pointer.

📁 File Transfer

Send files directly between your own machines over your local network. No cloud upload, no emailing yourself.

📶 Any Connection

Wi-Fi, Ethernet or a USB-C cable between two Macs. MacLinker adapts to whatever you already have set up.

🛡️ Works With VPNs

Unlike many alternatives, MacLinker keeps working while a VPN is active, so you never have to disconnect to share a screen.

🧭 Menu Bar App

Everything is controlled from a small menu bar icon. Pair a machine, rearrange screens or pause sharing in two clicks.

⚡ Native and Light

Built in Swift and SwiftUI, including full support for Apple Silicon Macs. It stays fast and uses very little power.

🧭 Setting Up Your First Pair

With MacLinker installed on two or more machines, the rest is a short, guided process.

  1. Put your Macs on the same network. Connect them to the same Wi-Fi network, the same wired network, or join them directly with a USB-C cable.
  2. Open MacLinker on each Mac. The menu bar icon shows that the app is running and ready.
  3. Look for nearby machines. MacLinker lists the other computers it can see on your network. Select the one you want to pair with.
  4. Approve the connection. A short confirmation appears on the other Mac. Accept it once, and the two machines remember each other.
  5. Arrange your screens. In the layout view, drag the icons so they match how your Macs sit on your desk. Left stays left, and the pointer flows naturally between them.
  6. Start using it. Move your pointer across the screen edge, and your keyboard and mouse take over the other Mac automatically.

You can adjust the screen order at any time from the menu bar. Nothing is permanent — if you rearrange your desk, just rearrange the layout to match.

🔧 Tips and Troubleshooting

My other Mac doesn't appear in the list

Confirm both machines are running MacLinker and are connected to the same network. If you use a VPN, MacLinker is designed to keep working with it, but make sure the app has been allowed through your computer's security prompts the first time it runs.

The pointer feels laggy

Wireless connections share bandwidth with everything else on your network. If you have a USB-C cable or an Ethernet cable available, using it gives the smoothest, fastest experience. Large file transfers are also much quicker over a cable.

Clipboard sync isn't working

Check that the two machines are still shown as connected in the menu bar. If either machine has been asleep, wake it, wait a moment, and the link should come back on its own.

I want to pause sharing for a while

Open the menu bar icon and switch sharing off. Your pairing is remembered, so you can turn it back on later without repeating the setup.

I want to remove a Mac completely

Open the menu bar list of paired machines, select the one you no longer need, and remove it. Do the same on the other Mac so neither side keeps an old entry.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is MacLinker free?

Yes. It is completely free and open source. There is no trial period, no paid tier and no account to sign up for.

Do I need an internet connection?

No. MacLinker works on your own local network. Once the app is downloaded, the link between your machines does not depend on the internet at all.

Does it work without installing anything on the second Mac?

Every Mac you want to link needs MacLinker installed. The download is the same for each machine.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. Your keyboard, mouse, clipboard and file transfers travel directly between your own computers. Nothing passes through an outside server.

What is the difference between this and Universal Control?

MacLinker is an open-source alternative to Universal Control with the same core idea: one keyboard and mouse across multiple Macs. The difference is that MacLinker works over Wi-Fi, Ethernet or a USB-C cable, and keeps working when a VPN is running.

How do I update to a newer version?

Return to the download page, download the newest release, and run it. Your existing pairings stay in place.

🛡️ Privacy and Open Source

MacLinker is open source, which means its code is publicly visible and anyone can review exactly what it does. Nothing is hidden.

Because the app runs entirely on your own machines and your own local network, your keystrokes, clipboard contents and files never leave your control. There are no analytics trackers, no advertising and no cloud storage involved in linking your Macs together.

If you prefer installing through package managers, the project is also distributed as a Homebrew Cask, which is handy for anyone who already uses Homebrew to keep apps up to date.
